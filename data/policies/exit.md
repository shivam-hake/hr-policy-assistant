# Exit and Notice Period Policy

## 1. Resignation

### 1.1 How to resign
Employees resign by submitting a resignation in the HR portal and informing their reporting manager. The last working day is calculated from the date the resignation is submitted in the portal.

### 1.2 Withdrawing a resignation
A resignation can be withdrawn within 7 days of submission, with the agreement of the reporting manager and HR.

## 2. Notice period

### 2.1 Length of notice
The notice period is 15 days for employees on probation, 60 days for confirmed employees at grades L1 to M2, and 90 days for grade M3 and above.

### 2.2 Notice period buyout
An employee may pay for any unserved part of the notice period instead of serving it, if the reporting manager agrees. The buyout amount is the basic salary for the unserved days and is recovered in the full and final settlement.

### 2.3 Leave during the notice period
Earned leave cannot be used to shorten the notice period. Casual and sick leave may be taken during notice with manager approval.

## 3. Before the last working day

### 3.1 Handover
Employees must complete a handover of their work, documents and access to a person named by their manager.

### 3.2 Returning company assets
The laptop, ID card and any other company property must be returned on or before the last working day. Unreturned assets are recovered at their current value from the full and final settlement.

### 3.3 Exit interview
HR offers an exit interview in the final week. Taking part is optional and feedback is kept confidential.

## 4. Full and final settlement

### 4.1 Timeline
The full and final settlement is paid within 45 days of the last working day.

### 4.2 Earned leave encashment
Unused earned leave is encashed at the basic salary rate, up to a maximum of 30 days. Casual and sick leave are not encashed.

### 4.3 Documents
The relieving letter and experience letter are issued after the full and final settlement is completed.
